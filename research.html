Dissertation Research

Mathematical Modeling of Cardiovascular Disease–Mortality Dynamics: A Data-Informed Framework with Physical Activity Effect Modification

My dissertation investigates the relationships among physical activity, cardiovascular disease (CVD), and mortality by combining longitudinal survival analysis with a compartmental dynamical model. The empirical component uses repeated observations from the Health and Retirement Study (HRS) to examine these processes among U.S. adults age 50 and older.

The statistical analysis uses counting-process survival data, time-varying covariates, Cox proportional hazards models, and extended Cox models to characterize mortality and incident-CVD risks over time. In particular, the analysis investigates whether the association between CVD and mortality differs according to physical activity level and whether selected effects vary with time.

Mathematical formulation

The population is stratified by cardiovascular disease status and physical activity level. For \(j=0,1,2,3\), let \(S_j(t)\) denote individuals without CVD and \(C_j(t)\) individuals with CVD in physical activity category \(j\). The cumulative number of deaths is denoted by \(D(t)\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{dS_j}{dt} &= \sum_{\substack{k=0\\k\neq j}}^{3} q^{S}_{kj}S_k - \sum_{\substack{k=0\\k\neq j}}^{3} q^{S}_{jk}S_j - \lambda_j(t)S_j - m_{S_j}(t)S_j, \\[6pt] \frac{dC_j}{dt} &= \lambda_j(t)S_j + \sum_{\substack{k=0\\k\neq j}}^{3} q^{C}_{kj}C_k - \sum_{\substack{k=0\\k\neq j}}^{3} q^{C}_{jk}C_j - m_{C_j}(t)C_j, \\[6pt] \frac{dD}{dt} &= \sum_{j=0}^{3} m_{S_j}(t)S_j + \sum_{j=0}^{3} m_{C_j}(t)C_j . \end{aligned} \]

Here, \(q^{S}_{jk}\) and \(q^{C}_{jk}\) describe transitions among physical activity states, \(\lambda_j(t)\) represents CVD incidence, and \(m_{S_j}(t)\) and \(m_{C_j}(t)\) represent mortality rates for individuals without and with CVD, respectively.

Survival-analysis component

Time-dependent mortality and CVD risks are estimated from longitudinal HRS data using extended Cox regression models. A general representation of the hazard is

\[ h_i(t) = h_0(t) \exp \left\{ \boldsymbol{\beta}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_i(t) + \boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\top} \mathbf{X}_i(t)\log(t) \right\}. \]

The estimated hazard information is then used to inform quantities such as \(\lambda_j(t)\), \(m_{S_j}(t)\), and \(m_{C_j}(t)\) in the dynamical system.

Statistical–mathematical connection

Longitudinal HRS Data → Survival Analysis → Estimated Hazards → Dynamical Model

A central objective of the dissertation is therefore to connect individual-level longitudinal hazard estimation with population-level disease and mortality dynamics.

Research Questions

Physical Activity and Mortality

How is time-varying physical activity associated with all-cause mortality among older adults?

Cardiovascular Disease and Effect Modification

Does cardiovascular disease status modify the association between physical activity and mortality?

Population Heterogeneity

How do mortality patterns and physical activity associations vary across demographic groups?

Data-Informed Mathematical Modeling

How can estimates obtained from longitudinal survival analysis be incorporated into a dynamical systems model describing cardiovascular disease, physical activity, and mortality?

Mathematical Modeling Framework

The mathematical model consists of activity-specific compartments for individuals without cardiovascular disease, corresponding compartments for individuals with cardiovascular disease, and a mortality compartment.

Individuals may transition between physical activity levels, develop cardiovascular disease, or move into the mortality compartment. The framework allows mortality rates and other transition processes to depend on physical activity and cardiovascular disease status.

I am studying both autonomous and time-dependent formulations of the model. The model can be used to investigate long-term behavior, compare scenarios, study intervention effects, and examine how changes in physical activity may alter cardiovascular and mortality outcomes at the population level.

Dynamical Systems

Scientific Computing

Numerical simulation, parameter exploration, visualization, sensitivity analysis, and computational implementation of mathematical models.

Health Data Analysis

Longitudinal data management, repeated-measures structures, mortality outcomes, epidemiologic covariates, and reproducible statistical analysis.

Broader Research Interests

My broader research interests include mathematical biology, epidemiological modeling, nonlinear dynamical systems, optimal control, scientific computing, survival analysis, and the use of mathematical and statistical methods to study public-health problems.

I am particularly interested in problems where mathematical models can be strengthened by empirical data and where statistical analysis can be interpreted through a mechanistic modeling framework.
