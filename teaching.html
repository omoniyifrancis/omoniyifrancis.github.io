Mathematics Education

Teaching

My teaching focuses on helping students understand mathematical ideas, participate actively in learning, and become increasingly independent problem solvers. I want students to move beyond reproducing procedures and develop the ability to explain why a method works, determine when to use it, and communicate their reasoning clearly.

Teaching Philosophy

How I approach mathematics teaching

01

Conceptual Understanding

I want students to understand the mathematical ideas behind a procedure rather than relying only on memorized formulas or algorithms.

02

Active Learning

I combine explanation, guided examples, individual practice, questioning, and discussion so that students take an active role in developing mathematical understanding.

03

Independent Problem Solving

Students are encouraged to justify answers, compare methods, identify errors, interpret graphs, and determine whether their results are reasonable.

04

Confidence & Support

Students enter mathematics courses with different levels of preparation and confidence. I maintain academic expectations while providing structured opportunities to practice, ask questions, and learn from mistakes.

In the Classroom

Teaching practice & assessment

Teaching courses at different levels has taught me to adjust pacing and explanation without changing the mathematical standard of the course. I often connect new ideas with concepts students already know and move among algebraic, graphical, numerical, and verbal representations.

When introducing a topic, I typically begin with the central mathematical idea, work through examples of increasing difficulty, and then ask students to attempt related problems. Questions during class allow students to explain their reasoning rather than simply provide a numerical answer.

Assessment informs instruction

Homework, quizzes, examinations, in-class problems, and short questions provide different evidence about what students understand.

When several students make the same error, I treat that pattern as information about instruction. A concept may need to be revisited, represented differently, or connected more explicitly to prerequisite material.

Instructional Technology

Technology with a purpose

I use technology when it strengthens mathematical understanding, improves feedback, or helps students visualize ideas. I do not view technology as a substitute for mathematical reasoning.

Mathematics & Generative AI

I am interested in using generative AI as an opportunity to strengthen mathematical judgment. Students can critique AI-generated solutions, locate errors, compare approaches, and explain why a proposed solution is or is not valid. The goal is to keep responsibility for mathematical reasoning with the student while developing the ability to evaluate computational tools critically.

Mentoring

Supporting student development

Teaching extends beyond scheduled class meetings. Office hours, tutoring sessions, and individual conversations allow students to ask questions, identify areas of difficulty, and develop strategies for improvement.

Rather than immediately providing solutions, I use questions and guided discussion to help students become increasingly independent mathematical thinkers.
