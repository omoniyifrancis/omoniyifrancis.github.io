About

Mathematics, data, and applications

My academic work brings together mathematical modeling, differential equations, computational methods, and statistical analysis to investigate problems arising in health and biological systems.

My current dissertation research examines physical activity, cardiovascular disease, and mortality through a combination of longitudinal survival analysis and mathematical modeling. More broadly, I am interested in research where empirical data can inform mechanistic mathematical models and where mathematical structure can improve the interpretation of observed data.

Current Research

Cardiovascular Disease, Physical Activity & Mortality

My dissertation, Mathematical Modeling of Cardiovascular Disease–Mortality Dynamics: A Data-Informed Framework with Physical Activity Effect Modification , combines longitudinal statistical analysis with mechanistic mathematical modeling.

The project uses repeated observations from the Health and Retirement Study together with survival-analysis methods and systems of differential equations to investigate mortality dynamics among older adults.
