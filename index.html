Applied Mathematician & Mathematics Educator

My research lies at the intersection of applied mathematics, mathematical biology, survival analysis, dynamical systems, and scientific computing. I am particularly interested in developing data-informed mathematical models for problems arising in population health.

My dissertation integrates longitudinal survival analysis with a physical activity-stratified compartmental model to investigate cardiovascular disease and mortality dynamics among older adults.

As an educator, I am interested in helping students develop conceptual understanding, mathematical confidence, and the ability to connect mathematical ideas with real-world problems.

Current Research

Cardiovascular Disease, Physical Activity & Mortality

My dissertation, Mathematical Modeling of Cardiovascular Disease–Mortality Dynamics: A Data-Informed Framework with Physical Activity Effect Modification , combines longitudinal statistical analysis with mechanistic mathematical modeling.

The project uses repeated observations from the Health and Retirement Study together with survival-analysis methods and systems of differential equations to investigate mortality dynamics among older adults.
